# Upgrading to eidotter 0.47.0

From 0.46.x. Coming from older versions, read the earlier guides first
([0.41.0](./0.41.0.md), [0.43.0](./0.43.0.md), [0.44.0](./0.44.0.md),
[0.45.0](./0.45.0.md), [0.46.0](./0.46.0.md)).

0.47.0 changes the default theme. The new default, `dos-86`, is amber text
with honest functional colours: errors are red, success green, warnings
yellow, info cyan. Component text is set in the body face, JetBrains Mono
Nerd Font; headings keep the DOS display face. Its CRT effects are calmer,
tuned for long reading. The
previous default is now called Pure Amber (`pure-amber`) and stays available,
unchanged; a site can keep it as its default with two lines.

## Who must act

- Sites that set `data-theme` from `resolveDisplayTheme()` / DisplayMenu
  without passing `dosDark`: the site turns `dos-86` on upgrade, with new
  functional colours and a new body typeface. Check pages (sections 2 and 3),
  or keep Pure Amber (section 4).
- Sites that hard-code `data-theme="amber-mono"` or `"pure-amber"`: nothing
  changes. Rename `amber-mono` to `pure-amber` when convenient (section 6).
- Sites that set no `data-theme` at all: nothing changes (section 5).
- Sites that mount `RetroEffects`: the effects calm down under `dos-86`
  (section 7).

## 1. How to bump

```bash
npm install eidotter@^0.47.0
npm ls eidotter   # 0.47.0 or later
```

Commit the lockfile. 0.x carets do not cross minors: `^0.46.0` never
resolves to 0.47.0.

## 2. Visible: the default theme is `dos-86`

**What changed.** `resolveDisplayTheme()` and `displayMenuItems()` default
`dosDark` to `'dos-86'` (it was `'amber-mono'`). `eidotter/styles` now
bundles the `dos-86` theme as well as Pure Amber, so
`data-theme="dos-86"` works without an extra import.

Under `dos-86`, compared with Pure Amber:

- Text, borders, links, the focus ring, the card surface and the AI
  provenance colours are the same.
- The ground is the DOS-86 screen colour `#050300` (Pure Amber: `#020003`).
- Functional colours are honest: error text, borders and status `#FF5555`,
  success `#55FF55`, warning `#FFFF55`, info `#00AAAA`; alert grounds are dark
  tints (`#430500`, `#122010`, `#352800`, `#0A1828`).
- The critical and destructive fill (`--color-semantic-background-error`:
  Button `destructive`, the health family's critical tone) is `#D00000` with
  white text (5.7:1). It is not a CGA colour: it reaches 3:1 on the screen
  (3.6:1) and the card surface (3.7:1), which CGA red `#AA0000` (2.7:1) does
  not.
- Warnings read as yellow everywhere a component signals one, including
  Alert and Notification `warning`, which use the brand amber under Pure
  Amber.
- The CGA hue primitives (`--color-cga-red`, `--color-cga-green`, ...) take
  their honest values, so your own CSS that uses a primitive directly follows.

**How to find it.** `grep -rn "resolveDisplayTheme\|displayMenuItems" src`
in your app. A call without a `dosDark` in its `themes` argument now resolves
to `dos-86`.

**What to do.** Look at pages with alerts, notifications, badges, form
errors, destructive buttons, progress bars and the health components. If the
honest colours are what you want, nothing else to do. If your own CSS paints
a meaning with a primitive (`--color-cga-red`), use the semantic role instead
(`--color-semantic-text-error`).

**How to verify.** With the theme switch at its defaults, `<html>` has
`data-theme="dos-86"` and an error Badge is red.

## 3. Visible: body text in JetBrains Mono under `dos-86`

**What changed.** Under `dos-86` all non-heading component text (labels,
buttons, badges, inputs, menus, body copy, card text, timeline labels)
renders in JetBrains Mono Nerd Font, the body face. Real headings, display
numbers (Stat, Gauge and VitalsStrip values), window and dialog strip
titles, the wordmark and the Header brand keep the display face, Perfect DOS
VGA 437. Terminal and CommandPrompt keep the bitmap face. The size stays the
one body size (16px).

**What this means on a page.**

- The body face is about 20% wider than the display face at the same size,
  so text wraps sooner in tight containers (narrow cards, table cells, phone
  rows, fixed-width labels). Check those at 320 and 390.
- The body face has real weights: anything set bold renders bold. The
  display face has one weight, so `font-weight` did nothing before. Remove
  bold you did not mean.
- A site that stays on Pure Amber (section 4) keeps the display face
  everywhere, exactly as in 0.46.

**What to do.** Look at dense pages and phone widths. Abbreviate labels
that now wrap (do not widen their containers). Use `font-dos-heading` for
your own headings if you want them in the display face under every theme;
`font-dos` follows the theme.

**How to verify.** Under `dos-86`, a Button label computes to
`"JetBrains Mono Nerd Font"` and an `<h1>` from `.dos-h1` to
`"Perfect DOS VGA 437"`.

## 4. Keep Pure Amber as your default (two lines)

```tsx
const THEMES = { dosDark: 'pure-amber' }; // pass to displayMenuItems and resolveDisplayTheme
```

```html
<html data-theme="pure-amber">  <!-- first paint, before hydration -->
```

Both lines are needed: the first keeps the menu and the resolved theme on
Pure Amber, the second keeps the first paint from flashing another theme.
Pure Amber resolves exactly as the default did in 0.46 (a test in the
package pins every colour, shadow and focus-ring value). LIGHT still picks
`amber-paper`: the default light theme follows the dark theme, so a Pure Amber
site keeps `amber-paper` exactly as on 0.46, while a `dos-86` site gets
`dos-86-light`. To offer both, pass `{ dosDark: 'dos-86', dosPure: 'pure-amber' }`
(DOS-86 by default, a PURE switch for Pure Amber) or
`{ dosDark: 'pure-amber', dosPure: 'dos-86' }` (the other way round).

A site that passes only `dosPure: 'pure-amber'` now gets the PURE row by
default (before, the default dark theme was Pure Amber itself, so there was
nothing to switch).

## 4b. Visible: LIGHT on a `dos-86` site is `dos-86-light`

**What changed.** `resolveDisplayTheme()` and `displayMenuItems()` default
`dosLight` from the dark theme: `dos-86-light` when the resolved `dosDark` is
`dos-86`, `amber-paper` otherwise. `dos-86-light` is `amber-paper` with the
body face (JetBrains Mono Nerd Font) for non-heading text, so toggling LIGHT
no longer changes the width of the header on a `dos-86` site (DisplayMenu
trigger 151px vs 144px at 1280 before). Colours are identical to
`amber-paper`. The theme ships inside `eidotter/themes/amber-paper.css`; the
new export `eidotter/themes/dos-86-light.css` points at the same file.

**What to do.** Use the helper: nothing else. A site that sets `data-theme`
itself from the helper gets the new name. A site that hard-codes
`data-theme="amber-paper"` for LIGHT keeps the old look and the old shift;
switch it to `dos-86-light` if its dark theme is `dos-86`. Pass
`dosLight: 'amber-paper'` to keep `amber-paper` on a `dos-86` site. Anything
that matches `[data-theme="amber-paper"]` in your own CSS needs
`[data-theme="dos-86-light"]` added.

**Still shifts by design.** `dos-86` to `pure-amber` (the PURE switch) and
`dos-86` to `light` (the DOS switch on a site with a modern theme) change
the text face, so the header changes width there.

## 5. Not changed: a page that sets no `data-theme`

The bare `:root` of `tokens.css` is unchanged. A page that never sets
`data-theme` renders exactly as on 0.46: neither Pure Amber nor `dos-86`, but
the token defaults (secondary-amber body text `#B87C1A`, ground `#020003`, a
mix of amber and honest functional roles). This was already true before
0.47: the default theme has always applied through the `data-theme`
attribute. To get a theme, set `data-theme="dos-86"` or
`data-theme="pure-amber"` on `<html>`.

## 6. Deprecated: the name `amber-mono`

`amber-mono` is the old name of Pure Amber. `data-theme="amber-mono"`,
`.theme-amber-mono`, `eidotter/themes/amber-mono.css` and
`dosDark: 'amber-mono'` keep working for at least two minors and select the
same rule and file as `pure-amber`. There is no runtime warning. Replace
them with `pure-amber`.

## 7. Visible: calmer CRT effects under `dos-86`

**What changed.** Under `dos-86` the effect tokens take the long-reading
values without a `preset` prop: scanlines 0.20 at a 3px pitch and still (no
drift), no flicker, aperture grille 0.06 (faint vertical red, green and blue
stripes), vignette 0.22 at spread 0.20, bloom 0.22 when `bloom` is on. Pure
Amber and the other themes keep the 0.46 values. Effects still render only
where you mount `RetroEffects`; nothing appears on a site that does not.

**What to do.** Nothing, unless you want the old monitor under `dos-86`:
redefine the tokens on `<html>` (for example
`--effect-scanline-intensity: 0.6`), or turn the grille off with
`<RetroEffects mask={false} />`. Do not add an effects toggle to a site.

**Glow.** The glow stays per element (`glowMode="element"`, the default).
`glowMode="sampled"` (glow sampled from the rendered picture, an SVG filter
on the html element) is opt-in under every theme: it costs scroll
performance on long pages without GPU rasterisation.

**How to verify.** Under `dos-86` the scanlines do not move and nothing
flickers; dev tools show `--effect-mask-intensity` resolving to `0.06` on
the overlay.

## Checklist

1. Bump to `^0.47.0` and commit the lockfile.
2. Decide the site's default: `dos-86` (do nothing) or Pure Amber (section 4).
3. Put the default in one named constant that feeds `displayMenuItems`,
   `resolveDisplayTheme` and the first-paint `data-theme`.
4. Look at alerts, notifications, badges, form errors, destructive buttons and
   the health components under the chosen default; under `dos-86` also check
   wrapping and bold in tight containers at 320 and 390 (section 3).
5. Replace `amber-mono` with `pure-amber` in markup, CSS and imports.
6. If you mount `RetroEffects`, check the calmer monitor under `dos-86`.
